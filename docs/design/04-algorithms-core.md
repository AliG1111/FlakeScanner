# Design: coordinates, filters, methods, statistics, motion (§6.1–6.8)

Part of the design ([summary](../design.md)). Section numbers are stable; cite them as §N.

### 6.1 Coordinates and the angle convention

| Quantity | Convention |
|---|---|
| Buffer | Video frames in the sensor's native landscape orientation (connection rotation 0). x = column (right), y = row (down). |
| Portrait picture | As Phone LiDAR: buffer x = top → bottom of the portrait picture, buffer y = right → left. Normalized portrait point (xₚ, yₚ) = (1 − y/H, x/W). |
| ROI | `NormalizedRect` in buffer coordinates (0…1); converted to pixels per frame size. |
| Buffer angle β | β = atan2(dy, dx) of a direction in the buffer. Because y points down, β grows **clockwise** as seen in the picture. |
| MD | β_MD = atan2(dy, dx) of the material's measured displacement (direction of travel). |
| Flake angle | θ = wrap(β_MD − β_flake) to (−90°, 90°]. The portrait picture is a 90° rotation of the buffer (no mirror) and equals the view from above, so this θ is **counter-clockwise-positive from MD as seen from above** (requirements §4.1). Reversing the travel adds 180° to β_MD and leaves θ unchanged. |
| Sensor readout | Rolling-shutter rows are read in +y order (to be confirmed by the device probe, P0.8), so travel along buffer y (right ↔ left on the portrait screen, Phone LiDAR's mount) is travel along the readout. |
| Drawn lines and boxes | Angles of a hand-drawn MD line or of ROI sides are computed in pixels: a normalized rectangle is stretched differently along x and y unless the frame is square. |

`AngleFrame` holds this conversion; a unit test renders a synthetic flake at a known screen angle and checks the sign, and the test-target mode checks it on the device (the printed arrow and "+" arc, P8 V5).

### 6.2 Pre-processing

1. Copy the ROI plus a margin of ⌈4σ⌉ px (largest filter σ) from the `420f` luma plane; convert to Float32 in [0, 1] (`vImageConvert_Planar8toPlanarF`). Results are only taken from the ROI proper.
2. Banding: flicker seen through the rolling shutter makes bands along the sensor rows, which pull angles toward the rows. How far depends on the angle ψ between the strands and the sensor rows, on how well the layer is aligned and on the strand width, not on MD as such: without near-dark rows, the lab's bands (E4, §6.13) moved *Edge orientation* by up to 3.5° on the mean and 10° on the spread and *Flake edges* by up to 1.3° on the mean, 2.0° on the spread and 4.9 points of in20, each at different directions, alignments and widths; on a random layer they narrowed the raw spread by up to 11.5° and 12.7°, so that the mat looked aligned. Every shot measures two values on the raw ROI (§6.10): the *common band gradient* (the slope of the row profile that all parts of the ROI share) and the *darkest row* (the smoothed row profile's minimum over its mean). The rules come from E4: 10 % sinusoidal bands of 40 and 300 rows, weak sines of 40 and 135 rows just above the 0.01 flag, 30 % mains bands of 1,100–2,700 rows and deep PWM bands (a light on for 0.5–0.9 of each period of 135–1,000 rows, seen through an exposure of 64–128 rows as trapezoidal bands, some with fully dark rows), on layers at 0–90° to the rows with κ 0–4, with the travel along and across the readout and on strands of 24, 36 and 48 px, each paired against the same mats without bands (8 images per case, 16 for the wide strands):
   - **Near-dark rows** (darkest row < 0.15): the shot is excluded for both methods with reason *Light bands*. Such bands moved *Edge orientation* by up to 14° on the mean and 25° on the spread (28° on the mean for a weakly aligned layer) and *Flake edges* by up to 3.4° on the mean and 12° on the spread, and the row correction cannot rescue them: dividing by near-dark rows left *Edge orientation* off by +4…+39° on the spread, often more than without it. At a darkest row of 0.10 the correction still left 0.8° on the spread; from 0.17 it worked. The control and the mains cases read 0.56–0.94. Dark content that runs along a sensor row inside the ROI reads the same way and excludes every shot: in the default mount a shadow across the line, such as a cross member's; with the travel across the readout a belt edge or a guide. The ROI should hold only the mat; the quality panel names the reason, and the user guide says what to look for.
   - ***Flake edges*** is excluded (*Light bands*) from a common band gradient of 0.0025. No correction keeps it within the tolerances: dividing the rows changes the edges it finds, whether by the row means, smoothed row means or a gain shared by all strips (that last one left about half of the 440 banded cases outside the tolerances). Bands too weak for the 0.01 flag still moved it by up to about 0.8° on the mean and 2° on the spread, so its flag sits lower: with the flag at 0.01, 60 of the 440 banded cases were outside the tolerances in the lab's exploration, at 0.0025 9 (the results of record). The price is coverage under steady light and, on layers along the rows, some accuracy (*Detection*, below); the flag is provisional until the probe's frames (P0.8).
   - ***Edge orientation*** is flagged from 0.01 and corrected in the run's regime, or excluded. *Rows*: every sensor row divided by its mean and multiplied by the ROI mean. *Smoothed*: every row divided by the moving average of the row means over 0.7 flake widths, at most 25 rows (band periods do not grow with the strands), because dividing row by row also removes the strands' own row structure. In *Smoothed* the shot's *Edge orientation* is excluded when its darkest row is below 0.5, where the smoothed means cannot follow the band edges, and, with a flake width of 30 px or more, when its common band gradient is 0.04 or more: the 25-row window then leaves about half of a 40-row band, which moved such strands by +0.3 to +0.8° in the lab's exploration. Below 30 px *Smoothed* is always used: it passed every 24 px case but two and cost less than *Rows* on every mat without bands. From 30 px the regime is *Rows* when the run's median ψ is at least 50° (wide strands that cross the rows), otherwise *Smoothed*.
   - **The run's regime** (flake widths of 30 px or more) is decided once, from the median ψ of the run's first 10 flagged shots that are not dark, and kept for the run: a confirmed MD change excludes shots until the direction returns, to the same geometry, or stops the run (§6.8 step 7). Until then the regime is *undecided* and flagged shots are excluded for *Edge orientation* (*Light bands*); a run with fewer than 10 such shots stays undecided, and `Summary` says so. ψ is the angle between the shot's mean direction on the row-corrected copy and the buffer's x axis (the sensor rows), folded to 0–90°, so it needs neither MD nor the mount. The regime is decided once, not per shot or over a rolling window, so that the exclusions cannot pick shots or periods by their alignment; every lab case also passed under whichever regime a median of 5 of its shots could pick. `RunAggregator` decides and keeps the regime; with a flake width of 30 px or more the analysis measures ψ and R (the mean resultant length, recorded only) on every flagged shot that is not dark (the shot's own result in the *Rows* regime, otherwise an extra pass on the row-corrected copy), and `Frames.csv` and `Summary` record them and the decisions (§7.4, §7.6).
   - **Results.** Of the 440 banded lab cases, *Edge orientation* ends within 0.25° on the mean, 0.5° on the spread and 2 points of in20 of its control, or excluded, in 434 (deep PWM 33 of 34, strand-direction grid 91 of 91, wide strands 236 of 240, regime boundary 74 of 75) and *Flake edges* in 431 (32, 90, 234, 75). Outside them, *Edge orientation*: PWM bands of 1,000 rows at duty 0.9 with the travel across the readout (−0.36° on the mean, within about one standard error); a random layer of 36 px strands under a weak 135-row sine (−0.70° on the spread); 48 px layers at ψ 45° and 35° under PWM bands of 270 rows (+0.29° and +0.32°, on the 1–2 of 16 shots below the 0.04 limit); two layers that neither regime keeps within the tolerances under a weak 135-row sine (24 px at ψ 30° and κ 3, −0.33°; 48 px at ψ 30° and κ 2.5, −0.30°). *Flake edges*: a mains band of 1,100 rows at 30 % and a 300-row sine in the default mount (−0.43° and −0.49°), where the exclusion, not the bands, moves the result: with every shot kept the bands moved it by −0.16° and −0.14°, but the 0.0025 flag keeps only 5 and 2 of the 8 shots, too few to tell a selection bias from scatter; and seven weak-sine cases, the worst 48 px strands at ψ 10° (−0.82° on the mean, +2.0° on the spread). P1.6 (*Edge orientation*) and P2.9 (*Flake edges*) repeat these cases with 36 images.
   - **Detection.** The ROI is cut into 6 equal strips side by side along the sensor rows, each spanning every row of the ROI, and the row-profile slopes of strips that are not neighbours are multiplied (§6.10). Bands are a gain of the whole sensor row and add to every product; the strands' own row structure differs from strip to strip and averages out, so on mats without bands the value stays near 0 at any ROI width, except where the strands run along the rows and so span every strip alike; a single profile over the whole ROI grows as the ROI narrows (with a fixed threshold it flagged up to every shot without bands at widths of 512 px and less). Without bands the 0.01 flag took no shot of face and random layers at ROI widths of 384 px or more along the rows and 0–12 % with the strands exactly along the rows (90° from MD in the default mount); at two flake lengths (256 px) up to 12 % and 25–44 %. The 0.0025 flag of *Flake edges* takes more: at 768 px up to 25 % of a face layer with the travel across the readout and 44 % of a layer along the rows, at 256–512 px 6–62 %. The darkest row of these mats stayed at 0.55 or more. These false flags fall on the shots whose strands lie most along the rows, so leaving them out is not neutral: in a check outside the results of record (one set of 16 shots per case, ROIs of 384–768 px, no error bars) the kept shots' pooled *Flake edges* result moved by −0.17 to +0.33° on the mean and up to +0.36° on the spread against all shots, and the +0.33° (a core layer at 512 px) is beyond the 0.25° tolerance. So the 0.0025 flag is provisional: the probe's frames (P0.8) decide whether it stays or applies only in runs whose light check or 0.01 flags show bands, and P2.9 measures the selection with standard errors. The user guide asks for a box at least three flake lengths along the sensor rows, which keeps the 0.01 flag's false flags low (not the 0.0025 flag's on a layer along the rows), and suggests *Edge orientation* as the primary method on such layers. Bands below the flags still act: in the lab's exploration weak sines just below 0.01 moved raw *Edge orientation* by about 0.4° at ψ 30–50°, and bands below 0.0025 moved *Flake edges* by up to about 0.8° on wide strands. A lower flag for *Edge orientation* corrects more shots without bands and failed more wide-strand cases in the lab's exploration (229 of 240 at 0.005 and at 0.0025), so it stays at 0.01. Image noise does not raise the value (E14: no false flag at 0.01 at any σ), but under bands it lowers it, because the value is divided by the image's own gradient energy, which noise raises: in a check of the final review outside the results of record (40-row sine, 6 mats) the value fell by 5–16 % at σ 0.035 and 20–46 % at σ 0.071 against the lab's σ 0.012, so on a face layer a band read at 0.015 would no longer be flagged at σ 0.071, which *Edge orientation* rates *Fair*. P0.8 checks the flags at the plant's ISO.
   - **Cost and settings.** On mats without bands the corrections are not free: dividing row by row moved *Edge orientation* by up to −0.63° on the mean and the smoothed correction by up to −0.40° (+0.28 to +0.52° and +0.12 to +0.28° with the travel across the readout), so they are applied only to flagged shots, and the 0.01 flag's low false-flag rate keeps *Edge orientation* within the tolerances. Banding correction (FR-MEAS-04): *Auto* as above; *Off* leaves *Edge orientation* out of flagged shots as well; *On* corrects every shot that is not dark as if it were flagged (for tests; *Flake edges* still follows its flag). Mains-frequency flicker without dark phases (a slow gradient across the ROI) moved no raw mean or spread by more than 0.25° at 30 % amplitude and is not flagged at 0.01; the 0.0025 flag takes some of its shots from *Flake edges* (3 of 8, *Results*), and the light check detects it (§6.16). Under banded light *Flake edges* keeps no flagged shot: the quality light and the conditions forecast (§6.18) name *Light bands* and suggest *Edge orientation* as the primary method. The rules rest on synthetic bands and mats: the 30 px boundary is untested between 24 and 36 px, and the darkest-row limit of 0.5 was set on 24 px strands. The device probe checks them on real frames (P0.8). Flicker-free lighting remains the real fix: banding costs shots, and a little accuracy that the rules cannot recover.
3. No contrast stretching or sharpening: both estimators are invariant to contrast, and extra filtering would add its own orientation bias.

### 6.3 Derivative filters

Gaussian kernels g(x) ∝ exp(−x²/2σ²) with Σg = 1, derivative kernels d(x) ∝ −x·g(x) scaled to unit response on a ramp, radius ⌈4σ⌉, applied separably with vImage and reflected borders: gₓ = d ⊗ g, g_y = g ⊗ d, σ_d = 1.0 px by default. Isotropic Gaussian derivatives avoid the orientation bias of 3 × 3 Sobel-type kernels. In the lab no rotation bias is visible above the sampling noise over eight directions (E8); the CI test of NFR-ACC-01 checks single straight edges and gratings at every degree: 0.1° for *Flake edges*, 0.2° for *Edge orientation*.

### 6.4 Method *Edge orientation* (structure tensor)

1. J = G_σi ∗ [gₓ², gₓg_y; gₓg_y, g_y²] with integration scale σᵢ = max(2 px, 0.25 × expected flake width in px).
2. Samples on a grid with step round(σᵢ) over the ROI. The margin copied in §6.2 feeds the filters, so no border band is lost; only where the ROI touches the edge of the frame (no margin, reflected borders) are samples closer than 3σᵢ to that edge skipped. At each: trace T = Jₓₓ + J_yy, M = √((Jₓₓ − J_yy)² + 4Jₓ_y²), coherence c = M/T, gradient orientation φ = ½·atan2(2Jₓ_y, Jₓₓ − J_yy); the edge (and flake-axis) orientation is β = φ + 90°.
3. Weight w = c·T (default) or, as an option, c with c ≥ 0.5.
4. Output: the weighted accumulator (§6.6) relative to MD and the per-frame 1° histogram (samples are too many to store one by one).

What it measures: the orientation distribution of all edges, weighted by edge strength — long flake edges, wood grain, and also the short **end edges**, which run across the flake. End edges make the distribution look less aligned: in the lab the spread reads +12.1° too high for 2.5 : 1 strands, +3.3° for 5 : 1 and −0.2° for 10 : 1 (E6), while the **mean angle is unbiased**. The method is therefore the robust cross-check for the mean angle; its spread is labelled *edge spread*, not flake spread. Because σᵢ follows the set flake width, the edge spread depends on it (E10: +3.3° at the true width, +1.2° at 1.25×, −0.4° at 1.5×, −2.8° at 2×); the mean does not (within 0.2°).

### 6.5 Method *Flake edges* (line-support regions)

Own Swift implementation of the principles of the LSD line-segment detector (Grompone von Gioi et al., IPOL 2012); the reference C code is AGPL and is not copied.

1. Gaussian pre-smoothing σ = 0.6 px; gradient (gₓ, g_y) as §6.3; magnitude ∣g∣ and level-line angle λ (perpendicular to the gradient, keeping polarity).
2. Pixels with ∣g∣ ≤ ρ = q / sin τ are ignored (q = 2/255 quantization noise, τ = 22.5°).
3. Seeds in decreasing ∣g∣ (1,024-bin pseudo-sort).
4. Region growing over 8-neighbours whose λ is within τ of the region angle (the angle of the vector sum of the members' unit vectors, updated as pixels join).
5. Rectangle: ∣g∣-weighted centroid; principal axis of the weighted second-moment matrix (the one closest to the region angle) gives the sub-pixel orientation; length and width from the extreme projections.
6. Validation: aligned-pixel density ≥ 0.7 (else the rectangle is narrowed, as in LSD), length ≥ 10 px, and the a-contrario test −log₁₀ NFA ≥ 0 with p = τ/π (binomial tail via `lgamma`).
7. Output pieces: endpoints, angle, length, width, contrast (mean ∣g∣).
8. **Collinear merging**: pieces whose angles differ by ≤ 3°, whose endpoints lie within 1.5 px of each other's line and whose gap along the line is ≤ 2 × the expected flake width are joined (union-find). The merged angle is the length-weighted doubled-angle mean; the merged length is the extent of the projected endpoints. Crossing flakes cut misaligned edges into short pieces more often than aligned ones; without merging the method under-reads the spread in dense mats (lab E7: percent within ±20° +1.2 / +5.2 / +7.5 points at three densities). Merging removes about 30–50 % of the spread error (E7, E9; §6.13).
9. Samples: merged edges are clipped to the ROI (the parts outside are cut off); those still at least the minimum length long (default 1.5 × flake width) are samples, which removes end edges. Clipping keeps the border neutral: in a geometric test (E12) clipped edges give the full layer's mean and spread within 0.22°, while counting only edges that lie entirely inside favours edges parallel to the ROI's long side (1.6° on the mean of a layer at 30° in a 3 : 1 ROI). Statistics are count-based (one sample per edge; a flake usually gives two edges with the same angle) or length-weighted.

**Flake width.** The minimum length (step 9) and the merge gap (step 8) scale with the set flake width (FR-CAL-07), so it must be right or too wide, never too narrow. With a true width of 24 px the spread error is +9.6° with the width set to 12 px and +3.7° at 16 px, because flake ends then pass as edges; −0.1° at 20 px, −0.6° at 24 px, and −0.9…−1.3° at 30–48 px; the mean stays within 0.25° (E10). The width is stored with the camera and format it was measured on, together with that format's focal length in px (fx) and the camera distance (§6.17): a format change on the same camera rescales it by the ratio of fx (a width measured at 1080p is half the 4K value), a new distance by the inverse ratio of the distances (the camera height varies from mount to mount: a width measured at 1.5 m and used at 1 m would otherwise be a third too narrow, which raises the spread by about 4°, E10 at 16 of 24 px), and a camera change asks for a new measurement. The width's distance is a LiDAR reading taken when the width is set (about 1 s, §6.17), never an earlier one, which may come from another mount: a width set at 1.5 m but stored with an earlier reading of 1.0 m would be rescaled to two thirds at the next Start (about +4° on the spread, E10), and at 36 px the cross-check does not catch it. When the width's distance or the current one is missing, Start shows the width with the distance it was measured at and asks the user to confirm it or measure again. `FlakeWidth` estimates the width from pairs of anti-parallel long edges (the two sides of a flake): for each edge the nearest anti-parallel partner on its bright side, then the length-weighted median of the distances. In the lab it is within +2…+9 % at 24 px, −12…−16 % at 36 px and +40…+77 % at 12 px in a dense mat (E11): good enough for a cross-check, not to replace the setting. The screen warns when the setting is below 0.75 × or above 2 × the estimate, so the damaging narrow side is caught (at 24 px a setting of 16 px is 0.61–0.66 × the estimate); at 36 px the estimate reads 12–16 % low and a setting of 24 px is not caught yet. P2.1 improves the estimate and tunes the limits.

The lab uses OpenCV's LSD as a stand-in for steps 1–7 and the lab's merging for step 8. P2.1 adds a Python reference of this section; from then the Swift detector is checked against it on the lab's fixture images (parity) and against the truth of Swift-rendered mats with the NFR-ACC-02 tolerances (accuracy, NFR-TST-02).

### 6.6 Axial statistics and confidence intervals

`AxialAccumulator` keeps Σw, Σw·cos 2θ, Σw·sin 2θ, Σw·∣dev∣, Σw·[∣dev∣ ≤ a] for each limit, n, and a 180-bin 1° histogram of dev = wrap(θ − target) (§6.7), all in Double; accumulators add. `AxialSummary` derives the values of requirements §4.2. dev = 90° falls in the last bin (89°, 90°]; −90° is mapped to +90° first.

Confidence intervals treat **frames** as the sampling units (samples in one frame share conditions, and one flake gives two edges). For frames k = 1…K with sums Wₖ, Cₖ = Σw cos 2θ, Sₖ = Σw sin 2θ: C̄ = ΣCₖ/ΣWₖ, S̄ = ΣSₖ/ΣWₖ; linearized residuals eₖ = (Cₖ − C̄Wₖ, Sₖ − S̄Wₖ); covariance Σ = K/(K−1) · Σ eₖeₖᵀ / (ΣWₖ)². For θ̄ = ½ atan2(S̄, C̄) the gradient is (−½S̄/R², ½C̄/R²), so var(θ̄) = gᵀΣg and the 95 % interval is θ̄ ± t·√var with t the 97.5 % quantile of Student's t with K − 1 degrees of freedom (2.26 at K = 10, 2.01 at K = 50, 1.96 for large K). Percent within ±a uses the same ratio-estimator variance. With overlapping shots (§6.11) neighbouring frames share material, so the interval is widened by 1/√(1 − overlap), with overlap capped at 0.9 (factor ≤ 3.2); above 50 % overlap the screen suggests the rate without overlap. Shown when K ≥ 10.

### 6.7 Histogram and rose

Histograms are over the deviation from the target, dev = wrap(θ − target): 1° bins internally (bin k covers dev in (−90° + k, −89° + k]); display bins of 1, 2, 5 or 10° sum them, and the axis is labelled in θ from target − 90° to target + 90°, so a core layer (target 90°) shows one peak in the middle instead of a peak split across both ends. The target is fixed for a run. The rose plot draws each bin at θ and θ + 180° (axial data), with MD along the 0° axis and the target direction marked. Means and time series are displayed in the same range (requirements §4.1).

### 6.8 Motion, machine direction and speed

1. Motion window: the ROI widened to a square, at most 1,024 × 1,024 px, downsampled ×4 (box mean) to ≤ 256 × 256 px.
2. Coarse shift: phase correlation of consecutive windows (Hann window, vDSP 2-D FFT, normalized cross-power spectrum), integer peak plus three-point parabolic sub-pixel fit, ×4 to full resolution. Range ±128 px at ¼ scale = ±512 px per frame interval in theory, reliable to about a third of the window (±340 px); 1,000 mm/s at 30 fps is 93 px at 0.36 mm/px and 185 px at 0.18 mm/px (0.5 m camera height). The window is at least 512 × 512 px even for a small ROI.
3. Fine shift: ZNCC of a central 128 × 128 full-resolution template searched ±3 px around the coarse shift, parabolic sub-pixel fit; quality q = ZNCC peak.
4. A reading is accepted when q ≥ 0.6, ∣shift∣ ≥ 2 px and 1 ms < Δt < 0.5 s (Δt from presentation timestamps).
5. Speed = ∣shift∣/Δt px/s, median of the last 15 accepted readings; × mm/px when the scale is known.
6. MD: the direction of the vector sum of the last 60 accepted shifts (longer shifts weigh more, as their angle is more precise); readings more than 3 MAD from the mean angle are dropped; standard error = circular SD / √N. *Detected* when N ≥ 15, SE ≤ 0.1° and the last 15 readings agree within 1° (modelled on Phone LiDAR's 12-of-15 vote).
7. During a run MD is fixed; the tracker keeps estimating. When the current direction differs from the run's by > 0.5° for ≥ 3 s (camera bumped), the change is confirmed and dated to its first deviating reading. A shot's rows and sums enter the run statistics and files only after a hold equal to the current time span of the MD window (its 60 accepted readings: about 2 s, longer when readings are rejected) plus the configured confirmation time (3 s by default), so the held shots from the start of the change are excluded with reason *MD changed*, like the following ones, until the direction returns within 0.5°. Frame images are written at once and kept even when their shot is excluded later, so only small values are held. Frame values still show at once; the run statistics lag by the hold. At Stop and auto-stop, held shots from the first deviating reading of a pending deviation on are excluded with reason *MD changed*, the others are added. A crash loses at most the held rows; everything written before is recovered (FR-RUN-05). After 10 s without return the run stops and saves with that reason (FR-DIR-04).
8. Line stopped: no accepted reading for 1 s or speed < 2 px per frame interval → shots pause (FR-RATE-04).

Expected precision: phase correlation on a rigidly moving random texture reaches about 0.05 px, i.e. 0.03° per reading at a 100 px shift and well under 0.01° averaged over 60 readings. The rolling shutter does not bias the inter-frame direction (every row has the same time offset in both frames). Synthetic moving sequences test NFR-ACC-03 in CI.
